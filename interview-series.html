The format

What an interview actually is

People are honest when honesty is safe.

Most career interviews are a performance. The guest arrives with a story they have already told forty times, the host nods along, and everybody leaves with nothing they can use on a Tuesday morning.

We push the other way. The pre-call is not a rehearsal, it is a negotiation about honesty: what you are willing to be specific about, and what is genuinely off limits. Salary, rejection, the manager who turned out to be right about you, the promotion that never came. Any of it can be in. None of it has to be.

You watch the cut before anyone else does. Anything you want removed is removed, and we have never argued about it.

Then you review. Not a summary, not a transcript, the actual edit. If something reads worse than it felt in the room, it goes. That single rule is why people tell us things they have not said on a stage.

Format
One guest, one conversation. No panel, no co-host talking over you.
Length
Thirty-five to fifty-five minutes. We stop when it is finished.
Where
Remote, over video. No studio visit, no travel, no lighting rig.
Before
A twenty-minute call to agree the ground, and anything off it.
Your cut
You watch it before anyone else. Removals are not negotiated.
Cost
None. We have never charged a guest and we never will.
