01 - The case, from your side

Your students are not short of ambition.

They are short of the dinner where somebody's older sibling explains which teams are hiring, what the interview loop actually tests, and that the job title they have been scrolling past is the one they would be good at.

Two students leave the same course with the same grades. One of them had that dinner. The other applies into a void. That is not a talent problem, it is a proximity problem - and it is the one thing a careers fair cannot manufacture.

We bring the conversation. You already have the room.

Everything below is the mechanics of getting the right practitioner in front of the right cohort, on your timetable, and leaving something behind that your advisers can re-run without us.

02 - How a partnership is scoped

Four steps, none of which need a procurement cycle.

Most partnerships start with one person inside an institution who already knows which conversation their students are missing. Usually it begins with an email, not a tender.

  • 01
    We talkA short call to understand your programme, your students, and what success looks like for your institution. No deck.
  • 02
    We shape it togetherInterview content, guest access, or a joint session, built around what your students actually need this year.
  • 03
    We run itOn campus or online, on a timeline that fits your semester and your assessment periods.
  • 04
    You keep itRecordings, materials and a named contact. The relationship outlasts the event.
03 - What it can include

Pick the parts that fit.

Most partnerships begin with one of these and add the others once a cohort has been through it. Nothing here is a bundle you have to take whole.

  • 01
    Guest sessionsA practitioner from the series, on campus or online, matched to your course mix. A talk, then a long Q&A, which is the part students remember.
  • 02
    Cohort workshopsApplications, portfolios and interview practice, run against how hiring actually works this year rather than a template from 2019.
  • 03
    A content library you keepCurated interviews, recordings from your own sessions and the accompanying notes. Yours to reuse with next year's cohort without asking us.
  • 04
    Co-branded interviewsWe record with your alumni and your industry contacts, and the episodes carry your institution alongside ours.
  • 05
    Panels and careers weeksA slot in an existing careers week, or a full panel built around the routes your students keep asking about.
  • 06
    Society collaborationsSmaller, student-led formats for computing, data and design societies, where the room sets the agenda instead of us.

05 - What your team keeps

The point is not the event.

A one-off careers talk is a nice afternoon. What matters is what is still usable in your careers office once the room has emptied and the cohort has graduated.

  • 01
    Recordings of every sessionRun with your students, hosted by us or handed over as files. Your call.
  • 02
    A curated interview shortlistMapped to your courses, not the full archive dumped on a shared drive.
  • 03
    Workshop materialsWritten so your own advisers can re-run the useful parts without us in the room.
  • 04
    A named contactThe person you speak to first stays on the partnership, plus first refusal on guests relevant to your cohort.
06 - Who we work with

Six people inside an institution.

And what each one usually wants out of it.

Departments and services

  • Careers and employability - sessions students actually attend, and something reusable afterwards.
  • Computing, engineering, data - practitioners who can speak to the course content, not the industry in general.
  • Business schools - routes into tech that do not require a computer science degree.

Access and community

  • Widening participation - first-generation students who do not have this conversation waiting at home.
  • Student societies - smaller, student-led formats where the room sets the agenda.
  • Alumni relations - a reason to call an alum that is not a donation ask.
07 - Cost and commitment

The questions careers teams ask first.

What does a partnership cost?

It depends on the shape and the number of sessions. The first conversation is free, and we will give you the number on that call rather than after three meetings and a proposal document.

Do students pay anything?

Never. The interviews are open and always will be. Institutions pay for the programmes we run with them, not for their students to watch a conversation.

How much lead time do you need?

About two weeks for a single guest session. Four to six if you want a workshop series built around a specific cohort, mostly because matching the right guest takes longer than booking a room.

Does this have to go through procurement?

Usually not for a single session. If it does, tell us early and we will complete whatever your finance team needs. We would rather do the paperwork than lose the term.

What if our cohort is small?

Twelve engaged students in a seminar room is a better session than three hundred in a lecture theatre. Cohort size is not the filter, and we have no minimum.

Who owns the recordings?

You do, for anything recorded with your students. We ask only that we can name the partnership when we describe our work. Guests keep their usual right to pull anything.

Tell us about your institution.

Two or three lines is enough to start. If it is not a fit we will say so quickly, rather than book a call to say it slowly.
