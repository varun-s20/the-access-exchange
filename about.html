Interviews For Universities
ESC

A proximity problem, not a talent problem.

Written by the founder · London · 2026

The Access Exchange started with an observation that is not subtle once you have seen it. The best career advice in tech is not secret. It is just unevenly distributed, and the people with the clearest answers tend to only reach whoever already knows them.

Two people can leave the same course with the same grades. The one who happened to sit next to somebody's older sibling at a dinner knows which teams are hiring, what the interview loop is actually scoring, and that the job title they had been scrolling past is the one they would be good at. The other applies into a void.

That gap is not about ability and it is not about effort. It is about who was in the room. A careers fair cannot manufacture that conversation. A newsletter cannot either. What closes it is a specific person being specific about a route they actually took, including the parts that went badly.

So we do two things. We record long-form interviews with people across the industry, pushed relentlessly toward detail: real timelines, real rejections, the salary conversation, the promotion that did not come. And we partner directly with universities who would rather bring that access to their students than hope it reaches them.

Nothing here is motivational. Advice you can act on this week is worth more than advice you agree with and forget by Thursday. If a story only works as encouragement, it does not make the edit.

We are small, the interviews are free, and they will stay that way. Institutions pay for the programmes we run with them. Nobody pays to watch a conversation.

The line we keep coming back to Access is a conversation somebody else already had.
Who we interview

Mostly people two to ten years in. Founders and executives are covered everywhere else, and they are far enough from the start that they have stopped remembering what was confusing.

Why guests say yes

Every guest watches their own cut before anybody else does, and anything they want removed is removed without a negotiation. People are honest when honesty is safe.

Five things we will not do, however tempting.

  1. 01

    We will not make a guest sound tidier than they were. Every interview is pushed toward the actual timeline and the actual rejection. A narrative that resolves neatly is usually one that has been rehearsed forty times.

  2. 02

    We will not put the interviews behind a paywall. Free at the point of use, permanently. Institutions pay for the programmes we run with them, not for their students to watch a conversation.

  3. 03

    We will not argue with a guest about a removal. You review your cut before anyone else sees it. If something reads worse than it felt in the room, it goes, and we do not ask why.

  4. 04

    We will not charge anybody to be interviewed. Not for a slot, not for promotion, not for a better edit. If that ever changes it will be written here first.

  5. 05

    We will not hand a university a one-off afternoon. Recordings, materials and a curated shortlist stay with the careers team, written so their own advisers can re-run the useful parts without us in the room.

Who is behind it

"I kept watching the same conversation change someone's career, and it was always a conversation they got by accident. This is an attempt to stop leaving it to accident."
Founder, The Access Exchange
