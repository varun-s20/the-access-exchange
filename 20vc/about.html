The short version

The Access Exchange started with a simple observation: the best career advice in tech is not secret — it is just unevenly distributed. The people with the clearest answers about how to break in, what to learn, and what actually matters tend to only reach whoever already knows them.

That is not a talent problem. It is a proximity problem. Two people can leave the same course with the same grades, and the one who happened to sit next to someone’s older sibling at a dinner knows which teams are hiring, what the interview loop looks like, and that the job title they have been ignoring is the one they would be good at. The other one applies into a void.

Access is a conversation somebody else already had.

We built The Access Exchange to close that gap. First through honest, long-form interviews with people across the industry — the routes that went sideways, the rejections, the salary conversations, the parts nobody puts on a conference stage. Then through direct partnerships with universities who want to bring that access to their students rather than hope it reaches them.

Nothing here is motivational. The interviews are specific on purpose: real timelines, real numbers, real mistakes. Advice you can act on this week is worth more than advice you agree with and forget.

Why now

The interview series and our university partnerships are not two projects. They are the same idea aimed at two audiences: professionals who want the real story, and students who have not had the chance to hear it yet.

How we work

Specific over inspiring

Every interview is pushed towards detail: the actual timeline, the actual rejection, the actual conversation that changed something. If a story only works as encouragement, it does not make the edit.

Mid-career, not just the summit

Founders and executives are well covered elsewhere. Most of our guests are two to ten years in — close enough to the start that they still remember what was confusing.

Guests choose what stays in

People are honest when honesty is safe. Every guest reviews their cut before it goes out, and anything they want removed is removed, no negotiation.

Partnerships outlive the event

A one-off careers talk is a nice afternoon. We hand over recordings and materials so a careers team can re-run the useful parts without us in the room.

Free at the point of use for students

The interviews stay open. Institutions pay for the programmes we run with them, not for their students to watch a conversation.
